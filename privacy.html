Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 24, 2026 · Applies to the Flash & Say app for iPhone and iPad (“the app”), published by Greg Alton (“we”, “us”).

The short version: Flash & Say does not collect, store on our servers, share or sell any personal information — from children or anyone else. There are no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no third-party software development kits (SDKs). Practice progress is saved only on your device.

1. Information we collect

None. We do not collect personal information, usage data, device identifiers, location, contacts, photos or any other data. The app has no sign-up or login, and it does not ask for a name, email address, birthday or any other personal detail.

2. Information stored on your device

To show progress, the app saves the following only on the device it is used on, using Apple's on-device storage:

  • practice sessions (date, level, how many items were read, first-try results, fastest flash time);
  • the practiced words and phrases and how many tries each took;
  • optional “how do you feel about reading?” emoji check-ins;
  • app settings (for example text size, letter spacing, colors and sound options).

We cannot see or access this information. It is never sent to us or to anyone else by the app. You can delete it at any time in the app (Grown-ups › Reset all progress) or by deleting the app.

3. iCloud

The app does not use iCloud. A future iCloud sync option may be added; if it is, it will be off by default, can only be turned on by a grown-up, and would store data in the family's own private iCloud account (not on our servers). We will update this policy before any such change.

4. Microphone and speech recognition (optional)

By default the app uses typing only. A grown-up can turn on “Allow answering by voice” in the Grown-ups area. If it is turned on, and the device supports it:

  • speech is recognized entirely on the device using Apple's on-device speech recognition. The app requires on-device processing; if a device cannot recognize speech on-device, the microphone option stays off;
  • audio is used only for the moment it takes to check the word or phrase, and is never recorded, saved or transmitted — not to us, not to Apple, not to anyone;
  • only the resulting “correct / try again” outcome is used for progress. The words heard are not stored.

Microphone and speech-recognition access can be turned off at any time in the app or in the device's Settings.

5. “Hear it” (read aloud)

The “Hear it” feature uses the device's built-in text-to-speech voices. Nothing is sent anywhere.

6. Advertising, analytics and third parties

The app contains no advertising, no analytics or tracking, and no third-party SDKs. It does not link out to websites or offer purchases inside the children's experience. We do not track users across apps or websites.

7. PDF progress reports

In the Grown-ups area, a parent or tutor can create a PDF progress report. It is created on the device and only leaves the device if the grown-up chooses to share it (for example by email or AirDrop) using the standard iOS share sheet. We never receive it.

8. Children's privacy (COPPA)

Flash & Say is designed for children and families. We comply with the U.S. Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) and similar laws by not collecting any personal information from children. Because no personal information is collected, there is nothing for us to review, share or delete; all practice data stays on the device under the family's control. The grown-ups area is protected by a parental gate.

Parents and guardians can contact us at any time with questions using the details below.

9. Data security and retention

On-device data is protected by the security features of iOS and remains until you reset progress in the app or delete the app. We do not keep any copies.

10. Changes to this policy

If we change how the app handles information, we will update this page and the effective date before the change takes effect in the app. Any change that would involve collecting information from children would require verifiable parental consent first.
